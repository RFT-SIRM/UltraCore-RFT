# Formal Contact and Research Submission Policy

**Purpose:** provide a clear, traceable route for research correspondence, collaboration proposals, licensing enquiries, and formal notices.

**Maintainer:** Evgeniy Zaprudnov (RFT-SIRM)

Use this address for research correspondence and technical questions. Do not send passwords, access tokens, private keys, or other secrets by email. Listing this address identifies a research contact; it does not by itself designate a legal-notice address or establish any institutional affiliation.

## 2. What to include

For a research or collaboration proposal, include:

- **Subject:** a concise description of the request.
- **Sender:** full name and, where relevant, organization and role.
- **Purpose:** the specific question, proposal, or decision requested.
- **Evidence:** relevant repository URL, commit hash, issue, paper, or reproducible example.
- **Scope and timing:** expected deliverables and any proposed deadline.
- **Capacity:** whether the message is personal, academic, commercial, or on behalf of an organization.

For technical defects, public GitHub Issues or Pull Requests are usually preferable when the information is safe to disclose publicly.

## 3. Institutional and official correspondence

For messages claiming to represent an institution, company, funder, university, laboratory, or other organization, use an address on that organization's official domain where available, and clearly state the sender's role and authority.

An official-domain address is a useful verification signal, not conclusive proof of identity, authority, or endorsement. Material proposals may require independent verification through a publicly listed institutional contact, written authorization, or a signed agreement.

RFT-SIRM may request additional verification before treating a message as an authorized institutional proposal. No partnership, affiliation, endorsement, contract, or commitment is created merely by sending an email, opening an issue, or exchanging informal messages.

## 4. Confidentiality and sensitive information

Do not post passwords, access tokens, private keys, personal data, confidential source code, unpublished sensitive material, or contractual documents in public issues or pull requests.

Email by itself does not guarantee confidentiality, privilege, or a binding agreement. If a proposal requires confidentiality, intellectual-property terms, payment, exclusivity, data access, or other binding commitments, agree on the appropriate written terms before sharing protected information or beginning the work.

## 5. Intellectual property and publication record

When discussing a contribution, identify the relevant repository, file, version, and commit. Keep research claims, implementation evidence, proposed applications, and commercial proposals distinct.

Public availability of a repository does not by itself grant rights beyond the license stated in that repository. Each project may have its own license and contribution terms. Do not assume the license of UltraCore-RFT applies to other repositories.

Do not describe a research result as independently validated, institutionally approved, or endorsed unless the supporting evidence is public and specific. Negative results, open questions, and limitations should remain part of the technical record.

## 6. Response and review

Receipt of a message does not guarantee acceptance, a response by a particular date, or an agreement to collaborate. Technical proposals may be redirected to public issues or pull requests for transparent review. Formal commercial, legal, or institutional commitments require explicit written agreement by an authorized party.

## 7. Not legal advice

This policy is an operational communication guideline, not legal advice, a contract, a non-disclosure agreement, an IP assignment, or a substitute for jurisdiction-specific legal review. Before making legal claims, sending formal legal notices, or entering a commercial or institutional agreement, obtain advice from a qualified lawyer as appropriate.
