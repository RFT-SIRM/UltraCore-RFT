    Research Map: Mathematical Foundations, Evidence, and Applications¶

    Evidence-first reading order (not historical chronology): dissertation and mathematical theorem → formal evidence → higher-moment research → separate spectral-decimation programme → systems methodology → implementations and applications.

    Historical origin: the maintainer-reported sequence is Rift Network → Rift-L1 Blockchain → UltraCore-RFT and supporting systems → Phi-Genesis → Evgeny-Theorem → Evgeny's Theory of Everything. See the origin chronology; exact dates are kept separate unless verified from source history.

    🟢 Featured dissertation: Recursive Graph Operators, Spectral Trace Defects, and Higher-Moment Recurrence. This research monograph separates formal results, finite certificates, numerical evidence, and open questions; it does not claim a completed physical Theory of Everything.

    This map is deliberately organized by evidence and research relationships, not by the historical order in which the repositories were created. The title Evgeny's Theory of Everything names a broad research programme, not a claim that a complete physical theory of the Universe has been established.

    1. The programme at a glance¶

    \[ \boxed{\text{Definitions and theorem}} \longrightarrow \boxed{\text{Reproducible evidence}} \longrightarrow \boxed{\text{Open extensions}} \longrightarrow \boxed{\text{Engineering applications}} \]

    The arrows describe an evidence-first reading order, not a theorem asserting that every downstream application follows from the mathematical result.

    Layer Programme Status and boundary
    1. Mathematical anchor Evgeny's Theorem Source repository reports a Lean 4 proof of a specific fourth-moment identity for concrete SU(2) operators
    2. Recursive extension Evgeny's Theory of Everything Finite-level certificates and numerical holdouts for higher moments; all-level recurrence remains open
    3. Separate spectral model Phi-Genesis Signed-flux Laplacians, decimation audit, boundary/corner corrections; Gate A remains open
    4. Systems methodology SIRM foundations Runtime state invariants and deterministic transition model; not proved by the spectral theorem
    5. Implementations Rift-L1, Rift-Network, seL4 report Implementation-specific tests, fuzzing, audit, and deployment evidence
    6. Exploratory applications RFT-QPU-Sierpinski, RFT-Invariant-Battery, RFT-Cosmology-Genesis QPU is pre-experimental; battery is a modelled energy scenario; cosmology is a reduced phenomenological model without observational likelihood validation

    UltraCore-RFT is being developed as the central observability layer for cross-repository CI. The Ecosystem Control Plane documents the registered projects, status meanings, run artifacts, and evidence boundaries. Its first phase reads public Actions status; it does not yet dispatch workflows in other repositories.

    2. Mathematical anchor: the fourth-moment identity¶

    For the definitions and assumptions documented by the primary theorem repository,

    \[ \Delta_m(H^4,\theta) =\mathrm{Tr}(H_C^4)-\mathrm{Tr}(H_{C'}^4) =-16\left(3^{m-1}+1\right)\sin^2\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right), \qquad m=1,2,3,\ldots. \]

    With the source's normalization,

    \[ I_m(\theta)=\frac{\Delta_m(H^4,\theta)}{3^{m+1}+3}, \qquad \lim_{m\to\infty}I_m(\theta) =-\frac{16}{9}\sin^2\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right). \]

    At the special angle \(\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}\), the limiting value is:

    \[ -\frac{8}{9} \]

    Algebraic sanity checks¶

    • \(3^{m+1}+3=3(3^m+1)\).
    • At \(m=1,\theta=\pi/2\): \(\Delta_1=-16\), dimension \(=12\), and \(I_1=-4/3\).
    • The limit follows by dividing numerator and denominator by \(3^m\).

    The source repository reports that the fourth-moment statement is formalized in Lean 4 for the concrete operators and the quantifiers in the formal theorem. A checked formal proposition does not by itself settle independent semantic review, peer review, or novelty relative to the literature.

    Primary sources: theorem statement · Lean formalization · verification record.

    3. Higher moments: what is and is not closed¶

    The Theory-of-Everything synthesis repository studies \(H^6,H^8,H^{10}\), Fourier coefficients, residuals, and a candidate recurrence.

    Supported within the documented finite scope: selected transition coefficients have modular/CRT certificates under explicit construction, denominator, norm, and coefficient-bound arguments; numerical holdouts and sparse-matrix-power checks are also reported.

    Still open: proving the all-level recurrence \(R_m^{(p)}(\theta)=R_4^{(p)}(\theta)\) for all \(m\ge3\), all stated admissible angles, and \(p\in\{6,8,10\}\). The exceptional first-transition correction also needs a derivation from the recursive graph/operator structure.

    A finite certificate proves its stated finite claim, not an infinite family. Small numerical residuals are evidence, not a universal proof. Read the dissertation, recurrence note, and modular certificate.

    4. Phi-Genesis: a separate operator family¶

    Phi-Genesis studies signed \(Z_2\)-flux Laplacians, spectral decimation, boundary/corner corrections, inertia computations, and exploratory mass-index patterns.

    • Gate A remains open while the full exceptional set and residual mismatches are not explained.
    • Tests support only the assertions and inputs actually covered.
    • A selected mass-index pattern is not an established predictive physical law without a mechanism, predeclared predictions, held-out validation, and independent derivation.
    • No equivalence with the SU(2)-connected operators is assumed. A bridge needs an explicit operator map, boundary conditions, preserved quantities, exceptions, and a proof or clearly labelled conjecture.

    Keep failed checks and rejected claims in the historical record. They are evidence about the research path, not cosmetic blemishes.

    5. SIRM: engineering model and invariants¶

    SIRM specifies runtime constraints for deterministic state transitions. The compact notation below is mapped to implementation variables immediately afterward.

    \[ \begin{aligned} I_1:\quad & S = B + Gp,\\ I_2:\quad & S = M - D,\\ I_3:\quad & d < p \quad (p>0),\\ I_4:\quad & E_i \ge -\frac{S}{10p}. \end{aligned} \]

    The effective-balance relation is

    \[ E_i = b_i + G. \]
    Symbol Implementation variable
    \(S\) total_supply
    \(B\) total_base_sum
    \(G\) global_field
    \(M\) total_minted
    \(D\) total_burned
    \(d\) dust_accumulator
    \(E_i\) effective_balance[i]
    \(b_i\) base_balance[i]

    Where \(\mathtt{effective\_balance}[i]=\mathtt{base\_balance}[i]+\mathtt{global\_field}\), under the model's integer and scaling conventions.

    The \(O(1)\) distribution argument concerns a shared scalar update and lazy effective-balance calculation. It does not imply every operation is \(O(1)\), nor does the spectral theorem prove the SIRM invariants. Runtime tests, fuzzing, audits, and deployment evidence have implementation-specific scope.

    6. Evidence vocabulary¶

    Label Meaning
    Machine-checked A stated proposition is accepted by the specified proof assistant and build configuration
    Analytically proved A written proof establishes the statement from explicit assumptions
    Certified finite-level A certificate covers the stated finite cases under stated bounds
    Numerically reproduced Computation matches a prediction on named inputs
    Engineering-tested Tests, fuzzing, audit, or deployment checks support an implementation-specific claim
    Hypothesis / conjecture A proposed extension awaiting proof or refutation
    Open A required derivation, proof, independent replication, or experiment is incomplete
    Rejected / not established Counterexamples or available evidence do not support the stronger claim

    A passing workflow proves only the assertions it executes. Fuzz counts and test totals are source-reported snapshots unless the page explicitly implements a live data source.

    7. Historical reading order¶

    1. Invariant-first engineering: define runtime state constraints and check them at transitions.
    2. Specific spectral theorem: state a closed fourth-moment identity for a defined SU(2) operator family; preserve the formal and numerical evidence separately.
    3. Higher-moment extension: investigate recurrences and finite-level certificates while keeping the all-level problem open.
    4. Separate signed-flux audit: test spectral-decimation and mass-index claims; preserve failures and unresolved Gate A cases.
    5. Applications: explore runtimes, protocols, kernel stress testing, quantum architecture, and energy-system models without treating them as proofs of the mathematical programme.

    This is a conceptual map, not a substitute for commit-backed chronology. Use UltraCore history, theorem history, synthesis history, and Phi-Genesis history for exact dates and changes.
